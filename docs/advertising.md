# Advertising: yard signs and door hangers

Researched 2026-09-16 against live vendor pricing, the City of Mount Vernon's own
permit forms and sign code, Skagit County Public Works, and federal postal law.
Every figure below has a named source. Prices were read from live configurators
on the access date and will drift.

---

## The finding that changes the plan

**Roadside advertising is essentially not available in this market.** This was
scoped as "signs by the road"; that turns out to be illegal on every road surface
around Mount Vernon.

- **Skagit County Public Works**, in its own published notice: neither state law
  nor county code allows signs in county or state right-of-way *"even on a
  temporary basis."* The same notice names the legal alternative directly — these
  signs *"are allowed on private property with certain restrictions and with
  property owner permission."*
  <https://www.skagitcounty.net/Departments/Home/Press/070912.htm>
- **Inside Mount Vernon**, off-premises commercial signs are prohibited
  (MVMC 17.87.110(A)). A cleaning-company sign staked at a roadside is by
  definition off-premises — it advertises a service not performed at that spot.
- The one commercial temporary sign Mount Vernon allows on public property is a
  **directional** sign of **2 sq ft or less and 2 ft tall**, two maximum, within a
  mile, carrying the site address, removed at end of day. A standard 18×24 yard
  sign is **3 sq ft** — already over that cap.
- **State highways** (I-5, SR 20, SR 536, SR 9) are governed by RCW 47.42; signs
  in the right-of-way are subject to removal by WSDOT.

**So the signs are job-site signs.** Staked on the lawn of a house she has just
cleaned, with that homeowner's permission, plus her own yard. That is legal,
it is what the county notice explicitly permits, and it is genuinely effective —
neighbours see it. The roadside-cluster tactic is not available.

> Caveat carried from the research: `codepublishing.com`, which hosts the
> officially codified municipal code, returned HTTP 403 to every retrieval
> attempt. The MVMC text above comes from a City-hosted document labelled
> "First Review Draft" whose wording matches the City's current A-Frame permit

## Yard signs — buy these

**25 × 18"×24" double-sided 4mm coroplast, full colour, with wire H-stakes.
Budget $200–$260 delivered.**

### Why 25 and not 10

The per-unit curve breaks hard at 25 and then flattens. This is the whole
economics of the decision:

| Quantity | My Campaign Store, 18×24 double-sided | 55printing, 12×18 double-sided, stake included |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | $145.00 — **$14.50 each** | $57.72 — $5.77 each |
| 25 | $189.00 — **$7.56 each** | $125.43 — $5.02 each |
| 50 | $244.00 — $4.88 each | — $4.68 each |
| 100 | $345.00 — $3.45 each | $436.52 — $4.37 each |

Going 10 → 25 nearly halves the per-sign cost for **$44 more in total**. Going
25 → 100 buys only another ~13%. **Ten signs is the worst value on the ladder.**

- My Campaign Store 18×24: <https://www.mycampaignstore.com/product/18-x-24-coroplast-yard-signs/>
  — but note this is **one-colour** printing and **15–20 business day** production.
- 55printing full ladder: <https://www.55printing.com/cheap-yard-signs/>
- Signs.com yard signs, 1 = $20.50 down to $16.40 at 5, free shipping over $99
  (a 25-sign order clears it): <https://www.signs.com/yard-signs/>

**Stakes are a separate line item at every printer except 55printing.** Real
vendor figures: **$0.97–$2.00 each** ($97–$200 per 100). Budget $25–$50 for 25.
An industry blog quoting "$2–$5 per stake" is roughly double what vendors
actually charge — trust the line items.

### Material, for Skagit weather

Coroplast is right, and **6–12 months is the honest lifespan** (up to ~2 years in
mild conditions). The counterintuitive part that matters locally: **rain is not
what kills a coroplast sign** — polypropylene does not absorb water. **Sun and
wind do.** So the risk here is an exposed, windy frontage, not a wet one.

Ask for **UV-stabilized stock and UV or latex inks**, and treat the 25 signs as a
consumable to reprint annually, not a durable asset.

## A-frame / sandwich board — do not buy one yet

An A-frame is only worth it with a **fixed business premises with street
frontage**, because Mount Vernon requires the sign sit **within 150 feet of the
premises it advertises**. For a mobile cleaning service working in clients'
houses it buys nothing.

Two further blockers if one is ever considered:

1. **The standard 24×36 A-frame every national printer sells does not comply.**
   Mount Vernon caps A-frames at **6 sq ft per face and 3 feet in height**. A
   24×36 face is *exactly* 6.0 sq ft (no margin), and a Signicade-type frame
   stands about **45 inches** — 3.75 ft, over the cap. Do not buy a 24×36 kit
   assuming it is permittable.
2. **A permit, a city-issued sticker and a signed Hold Harmless Agreement are
   required before installation**, plus a scaled site plan and a drawing of the
   sign copy. Portable signs are allowed only in C-2, C-3 and a defined part of
   the downtown C-1 area, must be removed daily after close of business, need
   15 ft separation from other portable signs, and — per the code document —
   *"shall be designed by a professional and fabricated by a sign manufacturer."*

**The permit fee is not published.** The Development Services fee schedule
(Rev. 06/2025, all 13 pages read) has **no sign-permit line item**. The fee is set
by MVMC 15.04.180's valuation table — the minimum band is $23.50 plus a
plan-review fee of 65% of that. Call rather than budget a guess.

If one is bought later, **buy a frame and swap the printed panels**, not a new
unit each time — replacement panels run $24–$36 against $119–$190 for a whole
unit. Aluminium frames last 10–15 years versus 3–5 for plastic.

|---|---|---|
| Signs.com kit (frame + two printed 24×36 panels) | $176.49 | <https://www.signs.com/a-frame-signs/> |
| Next Level: Signicade frame + 2 printed panels | $189.00 ($129 + $60/pair) | <https://www.nextlevelcustomsigns.com/signicade-deluxe-p/os-140-bk.htm> |
| Blue Wave, frame only | $119.00 | <https://bluewaveprinting.com/24-x-36-sidewalk-a-frame-only/> |
| Office Sign Company, frame only | $339.00 | upper end of the plastic range |

**Local shops, all quote-only, none publish prices:** Mortenson Sign, Mount
Vernon, **360.424.5099** (explicitly makes "a-boards"); Meyer Sign, Mount Vernon
(has a dedicated page on Skagit sign permitting — useful for the permit question
too); Skagit Sign Co., Burlington. No local price appears anywhere in this
research — every figure above is a national online printer.

## Door hangers — buy these

**1,000 × 3.5"×8.5", 14 pt gloss cardstock, full colour both sides, High Gloss UV
both sides, from GotPrint. $117.69 printing + ~$20–30 shipping = about
$140–150 delivered, ~$0.14 per piece.** Production 2–4 business days.
<https://www.gotprint.com/products/door-hangers/order>

The die-cut knob hole is included everywhere. **No printer charges a die-cut fee
or a setup fee** for full-colour digital.

### Why that exact configuration

**1,000, not 500.** The quantity curve is the whole story:

| Quantity | GotPrint 3.5×8.5, 14pt, 4/4, UV | Per piece |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | $36.69 | $0.734 |
| 100 | $54.54 | $0.545 |
| 250 | $75.24 | $0.301 |
| 500 | $93.24 | $0.186 |
| **1,000** | **$117.69** | **$0.118** |
| 2,500 | $205.19 | $0.082 |

Buying 500 instead of 1,000 saves **$24.45 in cash** and costs **58% more per
piece**. If the first 1,000 produce bookings, order 2,500 next — another ~30% off
per piece.

**3.5"×8.5", not 4.25"×11".** The large size is **+27%** at 1,000 ($117.69 →
$149.77) and buys nothing. The card carries a name, a number, "se habla español"
and an offer.

**14 pt, not 10 pt.** 10 pt is flimsy on a knob in wind.

**High Gloss UV both sides is the answer to Skagit rain, and it is free** — it
comes standard on 14 pt gloss at GotPrint, UPrinting, NextDayFlyers and
48HourPrint. One trade-off: **you cannot write on a UV-coated surface.** If the
crew should hand-write a date or a quote, pick 14 pt uncoated or 16 pt matte and
accept less rain resistance.

### Do not buy plastic

The measured premium is **3.1× to 3.5× at every quantity** — Vistaprint charges
$247.99 for 500 in plastic against $79.99 for the same size in glossy paper, and
plastic caps at 500 units online with slower delivery. UV-coated paper already
solves the rain at zero added cost.

**The "plastic hook card" does not exist as an advertising product.** Every
printer's "plastic door hanger" is a rigid plastic *card* with the same die-cut
hole and slit — there is no moulded hook. Genuine hooked hangers (claw and pear
shapes, 15–30 mil vinyl) are a hotel-supply item sold in packs of four and six;
no vendor quotes them at 1,000 pieces. The hole-and-slit card is the product.

### Cheaper alternative

Vistaprint, 1,000 at 3.5"×8.5" glossy paper, **$103.99 with free shipping** (over
$100) — the lowest all-in figure found. But that is a **sale price**; list is
$129.99 + $11.99 shipping, which lands within a few dollars of GotPrint. Its
advantage is design templates and a $10 design service.

**Perforation** (tear-off coupon strip): buy it at Vistaprint (+$11.20 sale /
+$14.00 list, flat, at 500), **not** UPrinting, where it costs **+$58.52 at 1,000
— a 42% upcharge**.

**Local:** no Skagit County print shop publishes door-hanger prices. Two
advertise the product and are worth a comparison call — **Copy & Print Store**,
1726 Riverside Drive, Mount Vernon, **(360) 416-3333**, and Anacortes Printing &
Signs. **MOO does not sell door hangers at all.**

### Shipping to 98273, measured

UPrinting, 1,000 pieces: Standard **$20.95**, Priority $89.89, Expedited $186.80.
GotPrint, PrintPlace and 48HourPrint all quote "excludes shipping" — add roughly
$20–30 to any of their figures.

## The law on distribution

**One hard federal rule: doorknob only.** 18 U.S.C. § 1725 and USPS DMM 508.3.1.3
make it a federal offense to place unstamped material **in, on, attached to, hung
from, or inserted into** a mailbox or its post. Fine for each offense.

Everything else is permissive:

- **Mount Vernon has no solicitor permit, no canvasser registration and no
  handbill ordinance.** Titles 5 and 9 of the municipal code were read directly;
  no peddler/solicitor chapter exists.
- **Washington State littering law** (RCW 70A.200.060) contains no handbill or
  advertising-material provision.
- A total ban on door-to-door distribution would be unconstitutional —
  *Watchtower Bible & Tract Society v. Village of Stratton* (2002).
- Honour posted **"No Soliciting"** signs as courtesy and complaint-avoidance,
  not because an ordinance requires it.

### Practical placement, beyond the law

None of these are ordinances. They are the standards that keep a complaint from
becoming one.

- **Doorknob only.** Not wedged in the door jamb, not under the mat. Those work
  loose, blow across the yard, and arrive at the city as a litter complaint —
  which is how a town that currently has no handbill ordinance ends up writing
  one.
- **Daylight hours.** Someone working doors after dark gets the police called
  regardless of what is legal.
- **Apartment complexes and gated communities are private property.** Management
  can refuse, and ignoring a refusal is trespass, not a handbill question. Ask at
  the office — many will say yes, and some will hand them out themselves.
- **Nothing on parked cars.** Windshield flyers generate far more complaints per
  piece than doors, and in some jurisdictions they are regulated separately.

**The one city requirement that does touch her:** a **$50 Mount Vernon business
license** under MVMC 5.04.030, required of any business operating in the city.
She needs it regardless. <https://ecode360.com/50286763>

## Letting the post office deliver instead — EDDM

**Every Door Direct Mail** is the USPS product for exactly this: pick carrier
routes on a map, print the pieces, drop them at the post office, and the letter
carrier delivers one to every address on the route. **No mailing list and no
permit are required** for EDDM Retail.

- **$0.26 per piece** postage.
- **200 minimum, 5,000 maximum** per ZIP code per day.
- Routes are chosen in the USPS EDDM online tool, which also shows household
  counts and basic demographics per route.

<https://www.usps.com/business/every-door-direct-mail.htm>

### The catch: her door hangers do not qualify

EDDM requires a **flat**, and to be a flat a mailpiece must **exceed** at least
one of:

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | more than **11½ inches** |
| Height | more than **6⅛ inches** |
| Thickness | more than **¼ inch** |

Maximum is 15 inches long, 12 inches high, ¾ inch thick.
(USPS DMM 201, Physical Standards for Flats — <https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/201.htm>)

**A 3.5" × 8.5" door hanger fails all three.** It is letter-size, not a flat. To
mail anything by EDDM she would need a separate, larger piece — a 6.5"×9" or
8.5"×11" postcard is the usual choice.

### What it costs against walking them

For 1,000 households:

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| **EDDM** | **$260 postage alone**, plus printing a larger postcard on top |
| **Door hangers** | **$145** all-in, delivered |

Postage by itself is nearly double the entire door-hanger campaign, before the
printer is paid. All in, EDDM runs roughly **2.5–3×** the cost.

### Which to use

The trade is money against legwork, and two things decide it for a business
starting out:

1. **A door hanger is alone on the knob.** An EDDM postcard lands in a stack with
   every other piece of advertising mail that day.
2. **Door hangers can be aimed.** She can hang the block around a house she just
   finished, where neighbours can see the work. EDDM buys a whole carrier route
   whether it fits the service area or not.

**Start with door hangers. EDDM is how to scale later** without hiring someone to
walk a route — and it is the right answer the moment her time is worth more than
the $115 difference.
