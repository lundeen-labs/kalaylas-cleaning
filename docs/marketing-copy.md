# Printed marketing copy — door hangers and yard signs

Bilingual copy, ready for a printer. Buying decisions, quantities and vendor
prices are in [advertising.md](./advertising.md).

**Three corrections were applied to the drafted copy before it landed here, and
they matter because each would have cost real money at the print run:**

1. The draft carried **`(360) 555-0123`** — a placeholder in the reserved
   fictional 555 range. The real number is **(360) 333-2732**.
2. The draft offered **"$99 first clean / 20% off"**. At her current rates a 20%
   first-clean discount on the Basic tier nets **$13.17/hour** — below
   Washington's 2026 minimum wage of $17.13. The offer is now her **free
   15-minute consultation**, which is real, already bookable on her Setmore
   page, and costs her nothing. See [operations.md](./operations.md).
3. The draft printed **`kalaylascleaning.com`**, which is not registered. The
   cards carry the booking URL that works today.

No price appears on either card. Her rates are under review (see
`operations.md`) and a printed price is the most expensive thing to get wrong —
1,000 cards outlive a price change.

## Terminology — what to call the work

This is the part most likely to quietly offend, and it is not a translation
question. Anything framing the worker as a household **servant** is class-loaded
in Mexican usage; anything framing it as a **trade** reads as respectful.

**Use:**

| Term | Why |
|---|---|
| `servicio de limpieza` | Neutral, describes the service, no class connotation |
| `limpieza de casas` | Plain and searchable |
| `limpiadora profesional` / `limpiador profesional` | Frames it as a trade, parallel to `electricista` or `plomero` |
| `trabajadora del hogar` | The legally-preferred term in Mexican labour-rights usage — but it reads like a job classification on a pay stub. Keep it out of consumer-facing copy. |

**Avoid entirely:**

| Term | Why |
|---|---|
| `empleada doméstica` | Formally deprecated in Mexican labour-rights language; carries a subordination connotation. Sounds like someone else's household staff. |
| `muchacha`, `sirvienta`, `criada` | Colonial-servant register. Actively offensive. Not even informally. |
| `aseo` | Industrial/janitorial-contract register (`empresa de aseo` = office buildings). Wrong domain for residential. |
| `señora de limpieza` | Not offensive, but diminishes a business to "the lady who cleans". Fine as something a customer says about you; wrong to print about yourself. |

### Regional vocabulary — Mexican/PNW, not Caribbean or Peninsular

| Concept | Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| to mop | `trapear` | `fregar` | In Mexican Spanish `fregar` means to scrub, to wash dishes, or idiomatically "to annoy" |
| to vacuum | `aspirar` / `pasar la aspiradora` | — | Both fine; `aspirar` is the everyday spoken form |
| bucket | `cubeta` | `balde` | `balde` is Caribbean/Andean/Southern Cone — reads as foreign here |
| broom | `escoba` | — | No regional split |
| mop (tool) | `trapeador` | `fregona` | `fregona` is Peninsular Spanish; reads as translated-from-Spain |
| deep cleaning | `limpieza profunda` | — | Universal, already standard |

Address the reader as **usted** throughout — `tú` on a printed card from a
business reads as overfamiliar.

## Door hanger — 3.5" × 8.5", printed both sides

At readable body type the realistic ceiling is a headline plus about five short
lines per side — roughly 35–45 words before it looks cluttered. Both versions
below sit inside that.

**Two versions, not one bilingual card.** Hang the Spanish-first card in
Spanish-dominant blocks and the English-first card elsewhere. A fully Spanish
card converts better with a Spanish-dominant reader than an English card
carrying a small "se habla español" line, because the badge still asks them to
parse an English card to find it. Print roughly half and half and adjust by
which blocks produce calls.

a customer searching for her needs one spelling. The phone number sits in the
identical position on every card so a scanning eye finds it without reading.

## Yard sign — 18" × 24" coroplast, double-sided

Legible letter height is roughly **3 inches per 10 feet of read distance**. An
18×24 board realistically carries one headline at 4–5 inches plus a phone line
at about 2 inches. That physical limit is where the word count comes from.

**Put English on one face and Spanish on the other — not bilingual on both.**
Two languages sharing a face halves the type size and neither becomes readable
from a moving car. A sign faces one direction per approach, so a driver only
ever reads one face anyway. Splitting by face costs nothing and buys full-size
type in both languages.

**English face**

```
HOUSE CLEANING
Se habla español
(360) 333-2732
```

**Spanish face**

```
LIMPIEZA DE CASAS
Kalayla's Cleaning
(360) 333-2732
```

Layout on both: headline largest at the top, phone number second-largest and
high-contrast at the bottom, nothing else. No bullet list, no offer, no address.
A yard sign's only job is **name and number** — the door hanger and the website
carry everything else.

Alternative English headline if brand recall matters more than the search
phrase: `KALAYLA'S CLEANING` / `Call or Text` / number.

## Trust signals that matter to this audience

These came out of the research as specific to a predominantly Mexican-American
readership, and several differ from what works in the Anglo market.

- **Personal referral beats any printed claim.** `Referencias con gusto` earns
  more here than a star rating, because word of mouth inside the community is
  the primary trust channel.
- **`Negocio familiar` and `local` outperform credentials.** A rooted, known
  business is trusted over anything that sounds like a franchise.
- **Speaking Spanish is itself the signal**, not a convenience feature. Leading
  with it tells the reader before anything else that this is not a business they
  will have to struggle through in a second language.
- **Price transparency answers a real anxiety.** Open-ended pricing that could
  become a surprise bill is a specific worry in mixed-status households.
  `Presupuesto claro antes de empezar, sin sorpresas` addresses it directly.
- **Ask for nothing beyond a service address and a callback number.** No
  language implying ID, documentation, verification, or "screening", and nothing
  that reads like an intake form. The card should read as a transaction between
  neighbours.
- **State that cash is accepted.** Removes a real friction point for anyone
  without a bank account or a card in their own name.
- **Offer texting, not just calling.** It lets someone reply on their own
  schedule and in writing, which many prefer over an unplanned phone
  conversation in a second language. Her number must be able to receive SMS.

### A note on the demographic figure

Earlier work in this project asserted Mount Vernon is "roughly one-third
Hispanic/Latino". **That figure is unverified.** The nearest confirmed number is
**12.4% for WA-02**, the congressional district containing Skagit County — a
much larger and more rural area than the city. Mount Vernon proper is very
plausibly far higher, since the Skagit farm towns carry the concentration, but
the Census QuickFacts page returns HTTP 403 and the ACS API redirects without a
key, so no city-level percentage was confirmed.

**The strategy does not depend on the number.** What is independently confirmed
is that **no cleaning competitor in this market serves Spanish content at all** —
that gap is real whatever the exact percentage turns out to be.

## Where a card or flyer can physically reach people

Confirmed Spanish-reachable channels in Skagit County:

- **KSVR 91.7 FM**, Mount Vernon — community station broadcasting bilingually,
  with explicit English and Spanish local programming. <https://www.ksvr.org/>
- **Catholic Community Services Farmworker Center**, 2727 East College Way,
  Mount Vernon WA 98273, **(360) 424-8655** — services in English, Spanish and
  Mixteco.
- **Familia Latina** at Brigid Collins Family Support Center — bilingual,
  bicultural parenting support.
- **Community Action of Skagit County**, Mount Vernon.

These are plausible bulletin-board locations; none has been asked whether it
accepts business flyers. That is a phone call, not a research question.

Commercial Spanish-format radio out of the Burlington/Bellingham corridor is
commonly cited for this market but no call sign or frequency was confirmed —
not printed here rather than printed wrong.

## Before the print run

One step cannot be done from here: **order a physical proof, or at minimum a
PDF proof at 100%, and check the point size and line wrap against the actual
die-cut.** Door-hanger artwork has a knob hole punched through the top third,
and copy set without the die line in front of you routinely collides with it.

A real one-star complaint against Vistaprint on the access date was illegible
door-hanger text, where the vendor's reply was that printing is fully automated
and the customer's file had type set too small. Supplying properly-set artwork
makes that a non-issue.
