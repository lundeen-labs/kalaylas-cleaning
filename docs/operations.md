# Operations: pricing, tax, safety, services

Research and adversarial review, 2026-09-17. Two verification passes ran against
the underlying research — one on the money claims, one on chemical safety — and
both found substantive errors in it. **What survived is below; what was killed is
noted, because the killed claims are the ones most likely to resurface.**

**Nothing here has been applied to the website.** Her prices are live on a
booking system that charges them. Changing the site to disagree with Setmore
would quote one figure and bill another — the exact defect this project already
fixed once. These are recommendations for her.

## 1. Pricing — three of seven prices should change

### What she nets, not what she charges

Gross rate is the wrong number for a solo operator, because nobody pays her for
travel, quoting, invoicing or supply runs.

| Service | Price | Hrs | Gross $/hr | Net $/hr, realistic Skagit route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic tidy-up | $100 | 3 | $33.33 | **$14.10** |
| Spotless | $160 | 5 | $32.00 | **$16.36** |
| Deep clean | $400 | 8 | $50.00 | $31.96 |
| Commercial <1,000 sq ft | $350 | 8 | $43.75 | $27.27 |
| Commercial 1,000–5,000 | $800 | 8 | $100.00 | — |
| Commercial 5,000–10,000 | $1,300 | 8 | $162.50 | — |
| Commercial >10,000 | $1,400+ | 8 | $175.00 | — |

**Washington's 2026 minimum wage is $17.13/hour.** Her Basic tier nets
**$14.10** — $3.03 *below* the wage floor — and that buys no health insurance, no
paid time off, no retirement and no sick day. A minimum-wage job does not require
her to supply a vacuum.

The model behind those net figures: 20 miles at $0.76/mi, supplies $4/billable
hour, $1.46/billable hour of fixed overhead (liability insurance $705/yr + bond
$112 + equipment amortization $450 + phone/software $480, over 1,200 billable
hours), 1.17 hours of unbilled travel and admin per job, then self-employment tax
at 14.13% of profit.

Worked, Basic tidy-up: costs $31.57, profit $68.43, SE tax $9.67, **net $58.76
for 4.17 hours of her life = $14.10/hr.**

Sensitivity, so this is not overstated: on a tight in-town route (12 miles,
45 min unbilled) Basic reaches $17.75/hr and Spotless $19.21/hr — barely above
the floor. On a dense repeat route, $20.50 and $21.33. **There is no assumption
set under which $100 for 3 hours is a good price.**

### The structural defect

**Spotless ($32.00/hr) earns less per hour than Basic ($33.33/hr).** Deeper,
dirtier, more skilled work pays her less. That is the clearest defect in the
schedule and it is independent of whether the absolute numbers are right.

### Recommended changes

| Service | Now | Change to | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic tidy-up | $100 / 3 hr | **$135 / 3 hr** | $45/hr gross, $25.76 net. Survives a 15% weekly discount at $21.13/hr. |
| Spotless | $160 / 5 hr | **$200 / 5 hr** | $40/hr gross, $25.18 net. Restores the ordering. |
| Deep clean | $400 / 8 hr | **unchanged** | The only correctly priced tier. Do not touch it. |
| Commercial <1,000 | $350 / 8 hr | **$350 one-off; publish $150–$200 recurring** | Keep $350 as the job minimum. Without a separate recurring rate she loses every repeat bid. |
| Commercial 1k–5k | $800 | **unchanged, scope in writing** | Healthy at $100/hr *if the day is real*. |
| Commercial 5k–10k | $1,300 | **unchanged, scope in writing** | Same. |
| Commercial >10,000 | $1,400**+** | **$0.13/sq ft, minimum $1,400** | Kill the open "+". As written, 30,000 sq ft costs the same as 10,001 — $0.047/sq ft. Unbounded scope against a bounded price. |

Worth more than any single price change:

1. **Set a per-job minimum of $135 and a service radius.** The margin problem is
   not the hourly rate — it is short jobs subsidised by unpaid driving. A 12-mile
   round trip versus 20 is the difference between $17.75/hr and $14.10/hr on the
   identical job.
2. **Quote flat, estimate internally per square foot, and never quote a
   first-time or heavy-soil house sight unseen.** Keep an hourly clause for scope
   beyond the walkthrough.
3. **Bill rented equipment as its own line.** A $100 rental absorbed into a flat
   price is 33% of that job's profit.

### What the review killed

- **"The $0.10–0.25/sq ft commercial standard"** — misquotes its own source,
  which says **$0.07–$0.20** and for *recurring* work, not one-offs. The
  conclusion that her small-office tier is overpriced is void.
- **Roughly twenty figures labelled `published-local-rate`** in the research are
  national aggregator averages. **Not one published Mount Vernon or Skagit rate
  appears anywhere in it.** HouseCallPro, ZenMaid, FieldCamp and CleanerHQ all
  re-serve the same Thumbtack/HomeGuide/Angi self-report pools — three sources
  agreeing is not corroboration when they share a source.
- **The 15/10/5% frequency ladder** is sourced to a study of Henderson, Nevada —
  different labour market, no B&O, far lower wage floor.
- **The allergy premium ("$55–$85/hr")** is one SEO blog with no disclosed data.
  Price the allergy tier off measured added cost instead.

## 2. Promotions

At her **current** prices, on Basic, middle assumptions:

| Promotion | Effective price | Net/working hr |
|---|---|---|
| List | $100 | $17.75 |
| 15% weekly discount | $85 | **$14.31** |
| 20% first-clean | $80 | **$13.17** |
| $50 flat referral | $50 | **$6.30** |
| Groupon (~75% off) | $25 | **$0.58** |

**Kill:**

- **Groupon and any daily deal.** $2.16 of net for a half-day of work — an 88–97%
  cut in net income per job, and it books months of capacity at that rate.
- **A $50 flat cash referral on residential.** That is **75% of the entire net
  profit** of a Basic clean.
- **"Free room" offers.** No evidence behind them, and mechanically a free room
  turns a 3-hour job into a 4-hour job at the same price — a hidden 25% discount
  on her thinnest tier.
- **Any frequency discount on Basic or Spotless at today's prices.** Every rung
  pushes her below minimum wage. Discounting comes from a healthy base.

**Safe:**

- **First-clean discount on the Deep tier only.** 15% off $400 still nets
  $28.97/hr — and commercially the first clean *is* the deep clean.
- **A $25 referral credit**, or a $50 one funded from a Deep clean ($29.95/hr).
- **Frequency discounts after the reprice.** Basic at $135 less 15% weekly nets
  $21.13/hr. That is what a discount should look like.
- **Gift certificates at list price** — deferred revenue, not a discount.
- **Bundling at full price on both lines.** A bundle that does not cut price is
  free.
- **The free 15-minute consultation she already offers.** Costs nothing, already
  bookable. This is the offer on the printed cards.

## 3. Washington tax — three facts that change the arithmetic

- **B&O tax is $0 for her.** Service & Other Activities is 1.5% on gross, but the
  Small Business B&O Credit fully offsets an annual service-class liability up to
  **$3,840** — which is **$256,000 of gross service income**. Below that she
  files and owes nothing. Any budget taking 1.5% off the top is wrong.
- **She must register with DOR at $12,000/yr gross**, not at some larger figure.
- **Sales tax — the gap that matters.** Under WAC 458-20-172, *janitorial* work
  (windows, floors, walls, in-place rug and upholstery cleaning, dusting, trash,
  bathrooms) is Service & Other B&O with **no sales tax**. Specifically
  **excluded, and therefore retail**, are: **special cleanup jobs (construction,
  fire, flood)**, **cleaning exterior walls**, chimney and furnace cleaning, snow
  removal, sandblasting. Those require collecting Mount Vernon's **9.0%** sales
  tax.
  **Before she sells post-construction cleaning or pressure washing, register for
  retail sales tax.** Quote $500 without adding 9% and she eats $45 per job.
- **L&I is $0 while she is solo.** A sole proprietor with no employees is not
  required to carry it; owner coverage is elective. It becomes mandatory the hour
  she pays a helper. The 2026 janitorial-class rate was not retrievable — pull it
  before she hires rather than using a guess.
- **PFML and WA Cares are elective** for the self-employed.
- **Self-employment tax is the real bill:** 15.3% × 92.35% = **14.13% of net
  profit**. With the standard deduction and the 20% QBI deduction, federal income
  tax is near zero at her likely profit, and Washington has no income tax.
- **IRS mileage 2026:** 72.5¢/mi Jan–Jun, **76¢/mi Jul–Dec**.
- **Liability insurance:** $705/yr median general liability, $968 for a BOP,
  $112 for a janitorial bond.

## 4. Chemical safety — ten non-negotiable rules

The adversarial pass found the underlying research **wrong in six places and
missing five dangerous combinations**, including the two most likely to happen in
the kit it recommended. Its verdict: *"do not publish this research as written."*

**These ten rules are what survived review.**

1. **One chemical per surface. Never two.** Not in a bottle, not in a bucket, not
   in the same fixture one after the other. Rinse and dry between products. This
   single rule covers bleach + ammonia, bleach + acid, bleach + quat,
   bleach + peroxide, bleach + glass cleaner, bleach + urine, and
   peroxide + vinegar.
2. **Bleach gets its own day, its own bottle, room-temperature water, and an open
   window.** Mixed fresh daily — it loses potency past 24 hours. *Hot water
   destroys the solution's strength and drives chlorine into her breathing zone*,
   so the instinct that "hot water cleans better" is exactly wrong here.
3. **Read the label of the product in your hand for the contact time, and keep
   the surface visibly wet for all of it.** If it dries early, you cleaned — you
   did not disinfect. Pre-clean first; organic soil neutralises the chemical.
4. **Disinfect hard nonporous surfaces only.** Porous surfaces get *cleaned*, and
   you say "cleaned". You cannot manufacture a disinfection claim on unsealed
   wood, grout, drywall, upholstery or carpet by applying more product.
5. **Label every bottle. Keep every SDS on your phone. Never decant into a food
   or drink container.** That last one is how a child in a client's home gets
   poisoned, and it ends a business.
6. **Gloves always. Eye protection whenever you dilute, spray overhead, or work
   above your face. Ventilate every bathroom.**
7. **Test every unfamiliar surface in an inconspicuous spot.** No acids, bleach,
   ammonia or abrasives on natural stone or quartz. No flooding hardwood. No
   steam on wood, laminate or delicate upholstery. Rinse chloride products off
   stainless.
8. **On carpet: less water, more dry passes, move air.** If it will not be dry
   within 24 hours you over-wet it. Low-pH rinse after an alkaline prespray.
   Treat any suspected wool as wool until proven otherwise.
9. **Refuse jobs needing training she does not have** — mould remediation,
   biohazard, hoarding, anything with blood.
10. **Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 goes in her phone before the first job.** If
    a mix happens: everyone out, do not re-enter, fresh air, call.

### The five dangerous combinations the research missed

Ranked by how likely a solo residential cleaner is to actually hit them:

1. **Bleach + a quat disinfectant.** Quats *are* ammonium compounds — this is a
   chloramine route. Most likely accidental mix in this kit, because the research
   put a quat concentrate and bleach in the same caddy.
2. **Bleach + urine.** Toilets, litter boxes, pet accidents. In a small
   unventilated bathroom — which is where the exposure statistics concentrate.
3. **Bleach + ammoniated glass cleaner.** The recommended kit listed bleach *and*
   Windex. Many glass cleaners are ammonia-based.
4. **Sequential in the same fixture:** a bleach cleaner, then an acid bowl gel
   ninety seconds later. The real-world chlorine event in home cleaning is almost
   never a bucket.
5. **Bleach + hydrogen peroxide.** Both were in the recommended kit. Rapid
   exothermic decomposition, caustic spatter.

Plus: **oven cleaner (sodium hydroxide) on aluminium** evolves hydrogen and
permanently pits the metal — and lye in an eye is a blinding injury, not an
irritation. **Never carry any product whose label lists hydrofluoric acid or
ammonium bifluoride** (some rust and wheel cleaners); HF penetrates intact skin
and a modest skin area can be fatal.

### Claims she must never make

- **Never "kills 100% of germs" or "kills all germs".** EPA's own definition is
  that a disinfectant destroys the organisms *named on that label*, at that
  dilution and contact time, and **not necessarily their spores**. A percentage
  printed on marketing is an unsupported pesticidal claim about a registered
  product.
- **Never advertise a steam cleaner as "sanitising" or "disinfecting".** A steam
  cleaner is a pesticide **device**, not a registered pesticide — it gets no EPA
  premarket review, and its Establishment Number *"does NOT indicate that the
  product has been reviewed for safety or efficacy by EPA."*
- **Never "removes all allergens" or any asthma-symptom-reduction language.**
  That is the precise claim the FTC sanctioned Oreck for. Use process language:
  "HEPA-sealed vacuum, fragrance-free Safer Choice products."

### Carpet — the failure modes the research omitted entirely

- **Over-wetting.** Latex backing delamination, seam separation, pad saturation
  the surface never shows, microbial growth within 24–48 hours. *The* error a new
  operator makes with a rented machine.
- **Wicking.** Dissolved soil travels back up the fibre as it dries, so the spot
  that vanished reappears the next morning. The fix is faster drying and more dry
  passes — never more water.
- **Browning.** Jute and cellulosic backings release tannins when wet, especially
  at high pH. The fix is a low-pH rinse and fast drying.
- **Air movement and dehumidification are part of the job, not an upsell.** If it
  will not be dry inside 24 hours, she over-wet it and owes the customer air
  movers.
- **Electrical load:** a heated extractor plus air movers will trip a residential
  15 A circuit. Separate circuits, GFCI in wet areas, no daisy-chained cords.
- **Furniture tabs under legs on wet carpet**, or finish and metal glides stain
  into the damp pile.
- **Between homes:** a rented extractor's recovery tank and hoses carry the
  previous household's soil. Full tank rinse, vacuum-only pass to clear the
  lines, disinfectant flush of the recovery path.

## 5. Services worth adding, in order

1. **Vacation-rental / Airbnb turnover.** Anacortes and the San Juan ferry
   corridor generate year-round volume. $75–$250 per turn, no licence or capital
   beyond what she already owns, and a single property can be cleaned 20–50+
   times a year versus once for a standard clean. Highest revenue per
   relationship, lowest barrier.
2. **Recurring residential and move-in/move-out for landlords and property
   managers.** Skagit has a large rental base; $120–$420 per turn. A few property
   management relationships fill a route without new marketing each month. Zero
   new equipment.
3. **Window cleaning.** Skagit's older housing stock means more, smaller-paned
   windows per house. $150–$350 per visit, under $300 of capital, and it upsells
   naturally to clients already on the books from (1) and (2).

**Skip as first adds:** carpet cleaning and pressure washing need $400–$8,000 of
equipment before the first job — capital better spent once the recurring base
exists. **Avoid** biohazard, hoarding and mould remediation: not because
Washington blocks them, but because the real gate is a written OSHA
bloodborne-pathogens exposure-control plan and a medical-waste disposal
contract, and the margins only pay at volume a solo operator will not see in
year one.

## 6. Allergy-aware cleaning

Position it as a **documented process**, never a medical outcome.

- **Equipment:** a true-HEPA **sealed-system** vacuum — Sanitaire FORCE/EON
  ($320–$464) or a ProTeam backpack ($695–$1,205). **The sealed body is the
  load-bearing spec, not the HEPA sticker** — a HEPA filter in a leaky vacuum is
  barely better than a standard one.
- **Products:** standardise on **fragrance-free**, not "unscented" — unscented
  can still contain masking fragrance. Cite the specific mark: **EPA Safer
  Choice** for a safety claim, **Green Seal GS-37** if a performance claim is
  also wanted.
- **Protocol:** microfibre, not cotton. Encase and hot-wash bedding for
  dust-mite clients. Any rented steamer or extractor must leave the surface dry
  within 24–48 hours or it creates the mould problem she was hired to solve.
- **Price it off measured added cost** — the product delta plus the extra
  protocol minutes — and disclose that basis. Do not repeat the "$55–$85/hr"
  industry figure; it is one blog.
- **No professional cleaning service currently holds AAFA's own certification**,
  so there is no established competitor claim to beat. Honest process language is
  genuinely differentiating here.
